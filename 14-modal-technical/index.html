the mechanism, said plainly

a system you can audit. not a black box you have to trust.

if the only way to defend a number is "the model said so," the number is useless on a live call. here is what is read, what the index is made of, and where it can be wrong.

what is read

  • the words the buyer says, transcribed live
  • how they say them — pace, pauses, where the energy drops
  • the deal context from your blueprint and CRM
  • the stage of the call — opening, discovery, value, decision, close — detected, not set by hand

what the index is made of

  • trust — does this buyer believe you understand their problem
  • urgency — is there a reason to move this quarter, not next
  • pain scale — how much this is actually costing them today
  • willingness to move — are they ready to change how they work
  • "in no hurry" — the model will say this out loud when it is true

readiness 0–100 is these signals, recomputed live. it steps in small increments — it does not jump.

where it can be wrong

  • on a call with heavy background noise, the transcription is less sure, and so is everything downstream
  • early in a deal, the buyer has shown fewer signals, so the index is wider-of-the-mark by definition
  • it reads the buyer, not the market — a budget freeze will not appear in the call until someone names it
  • a buyer who is quiet out of caution looks different from a buyer who is quiet out of disinterest. the model says which it thinks. it can be wrong.

saying what it cannot do is the most persuasive thing on this page.

privacy, by product design

calibration is yours. the deal belongs to the team.

the rep answers ten calibration questions once, privately, in their own words. those answers tune how the co-pilot talks to them — fast and direct, or patient and detailed. managers never see them.

the rep sees
their own calibration answers, their own coaching, their own readiness reads in the moment.
the manager sees
usage and deal activity. whether the call happened, whether the next move was taken, whether the deal moved. never the rep's answers.

this is a product promise, not a settings toggle. it ships that way.

for the vp of sales

four mechanisms. no invented percentages.

there are no published win-rate or ramp numbers yet — a fake number destroys the page for exactly the buyer it is meant to convince. what follows is the mechanism, in the manager's own language.

  1. 01

    win rate

    the same reps close more of the same pipeline — because the rep takes the right next move in the call, not the move they would have guessed at alone afterwards.

  2. 02

    ramp time

    a new rep performs like a good rep in weeks instead of quarters. the coaching happens during the call, where the buyer is, not in the monday review two days later.

  3. 03

    forecast accuracy

    buyer readiness is scored on every call. the pipeline stops being what reps hope and starts being what buyers showed — in their own words, on their own calls.

  4. 04

    coaching capacity

    one manager cannot sit on forty calls a week. this sits on all of them. the manager reads the verdicts; the rep gets the coaching.

your competitor's rep is going to have this. the question is whether yours does first.
