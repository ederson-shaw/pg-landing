Buyer Readiness Index™

MOVEMENT NEEDS EVIDENCE

A deal can move stages
without the buyer moving at all.

Seller activity is not buyer movement. The Buyer Readiness Index looks at evidence that a buyer may be becoming more—or less—ready to act. The number is not the product. The evidence beneath it is.

Six conditions reviewed in the evidence
  1. TrustCan the buyer rely on the seller and solution?
  2. ConfidenceIs uncertainty around the decision decreasing?
  3. UrgencyIs there evidence that acting matters now?
  4. Emotional safetyCan risk and consequences be discussed openly?
  5. Perceived riskIs risk becoming resolved—or increasing?
  6. Willingness to moveIs the buyer investing real effort in the next decision step?

What counts as movement?

Manager Coaching Report

COACH THE JUDGMENT, NOT THE SCORE

You don’t need another call score.
You need to know what the rep missed.

Managers cannot attend every conversation. PitchGenius connects buyer evidence, live interventions, readiness movement, and seller behavior to expose patterns worth coaching.

The management mechanism. Same-pipeline closing keeps the lesson close to the work. Live coaching happens during calls rather than only in review. Buyer evidence gives managers a clearer basis for forecast discussion. One manager can extend coaching beyond the calls they can attend.

Manager Coaching ReportPattern review / not a call score
What changed
Implementation concern surfaced late in the conversation.
What the rep saw
The seller noticed the buyer’s shift in pace and asked for an example.
What the rep missed
The concern was linked to decision ownership, not only product detail.
What blocked movement
No buyer-owned next action brought the implementation owner into the decision.
What to coach
Explore ownership before explaining the implementation path.
What happens next
Use the next call to validate who carries this risk internally.
These are not script mistakes. They are judgment patterns.
RECURRING JUDGMENT PATTERNS

Across calls, the same choices can quietly keep buyer readiness from moving.

  • Pushing too early
  • Explaining instead of exploring
  • Missing perceived risk
  • Mistaking politeness for commitment
  • Avoiding stakeholder questions
  • Failing to equip the champion
