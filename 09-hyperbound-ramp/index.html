For the manager

The arithmetic, as a mechanism.

No published numbers yet — so no percentages. Four mechanisms the manager can check against the org chart today.

  1. Ramp

    A new rep is a hole in the number for two quarters. Coaching that happens during the call, on every call, is training that does not need a trainer, a calendar or a recording. The ramp moves into the conversation.

  2. Coaching capacity

    One manager cannot sit on forty calls a week. This sits on all of them. The coaching capacity of the team stops being the manager's calendar.

  3. Win rate

    The same reps close more of the same pipeline. Nothing new to buy, no new headcount — the same people, with the next sentence ready when it counts.

  4. Forecast accuracy

    Buyer readiness is scored on every call, so the pipeline stops being what reps hope and starts being what buyers showed.

Your competitor's rep is going to have this. The question is whether yours does first.

Straight answers

What reps ask before they trust it.

Is this scoring me and handing the score to my manager?

No. Rep calibration is ten questions you answer once, privately. Managers see usage and deal activity. They do not see your answers. The privacy promise ships with the product.

Is this replacing me?

No. It makes you better at the human part of selling. It does not sell for you. The rep is the hero. PitchGenius is the earpiece — the thing that whispers the next sentence, then goes quiet.

How do we start?

Pilots, not self-serve signup. You book a pilot, we run it with your team on real calls. There is no free trial button because this is not a self-serve product.

Does it work with my call tool?

It ships as a desktop app — a small always-on-top card floating over whatever video call you're already in: Zoom, Meet, or Teams. Not a Chrome extension. Not a post-call report.
